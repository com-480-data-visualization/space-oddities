\section{Project Goal and Storytelling Approach}
The goal of \textit{Space Oddities} is to explore the historical growth and current density of artificial objects in Earth's orbit. We aim to transform abstract spatial data and raw tables, such as Conjunction Data Messages (CDMs), into a visceral narrative that highlights the risk of orbital congestion and the emerging Kessler Syndrome.

Our visualization follows a scrollytelling approach inspired by \textit{The Pudding}[à citer]. A single 3D/2D visual scene of Earth and its orbiting objects serves as the "backbone" of the story. As the user scrolls, the scene remains the anchor, but its state filters, zoom levels, and color encodings, updates dynamically. Each chapter introduces supplemental charts (time series, distributions) linked to the main scene to provide context without "data overwhelm".

\section{Implementation Breakdown}

\subsection{Minimal Viable Product (MVP)}
The core visualization consists of the following independent pieces:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Global Orbital Scene:} A high-performance WebGL canvas rendering the catalog of ~68,000 tracked objects as discrete points.
    \item \textbf{Temporal Growth (Chapter 1):} A scroll-controlled timeline that populates the orbit from 1957 to 2023, emphasizing the sudden "Starlink era".
    \item \textbf{Altitude Distribution (Chapter 2):} A transition to a structured view showing the extreme concentration of active satellites (89.5\%) in Low Earth Orbit (LEO).
    \item \textbf{Object Classification (Chapter 4):} A visual toggle to distinguish between operational payloads and debris, which currently outnumber satellites by 2.4:1.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Extra Features (Creative Enhancements)}
These features will enhance the depth of the project but can be dropped if necessary:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CDM Network Graph (Chapter 5):} A node-link diagram representing potential collisions where nodes are objects and edges represent dangerous proximities.
    \item \textbf{Interactive Ownership Deep-Dive:} Highlighting specific operators (e.g., SpaceX) to show their individual footprint in LEO.
    \item \textbf{Real-time Propagation:} Using SGP4 libraries to animate current satellite positions based on live TLE data.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Chapter 2: Orbital Highways (Altitudes)}
\textbf{Focus:} Visualizing the "stressed" environment of LEO. \\
\textbf{Sketch:} The view zooms out to show concentric regions (LEO, MEO, GEO). A side histogram of spatial density shows a massive spike at <2,000 km altitude.

\subsection{Chapter 3: The New Space Race (Ownership)}
\textbf{Focus:} Identifying which countries and companies dominate the sky. \\
\textbf{Sketch:} A bar chart of top operators. Hovering over "SpaceX" highlights their specific satellite shell in blue, while others fade into the background.

\subsection{Chapter 4: Payloads vs. Debris}
\textbf{Focus:} Humanizing the scale of "space junk". \\
\textbf{Sketch:} A pie chart shows the breakdown of the 68,148 tracked objects. A filter allows the user to view "Debris Only," revealing the cloud of abandoned rocket bodies and fragments.

\subsection{Chapter 5: Visualizing the "Invisible" Risk (CDMs)}
\textbf{Focus:} Making raw CDM tables (e.g., a 385 m miss distance) visually meaningful to non-experts. \\
\textbf{Sketch:} A "Danger Zone" view. The map zooms into a specific close-approach event. Lines connect satellites with high collision probabilities ($P_c > 10^{-4}$).

\section{Tools and Lectures}

\textbf{Tools to be used:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{React (Vite):} Component-based frontend framework for structuring the scrollytelling website into reusable components (scene panel, chapter cards, navigation). Vite is used as the build tool for fast HMR in development and optimised production bundles.
    \item \textbf{Mantine:} React component library providing accessible UI primitives (badges, dividers, layout helpers) without requiring a utility-CSS framework.
    \item \textbf{D3.js:} For linked statistical charts (cumulative launch area chart, operator bar chart, debris pie/donut chart, CDM scatter plot) and the scroll-driven year animation.
    \item \textbf{Three.js / WebGL:} To render the full SATCAT dataset (68,000+ points) as an interactive 3D orbital globe, and to draw the covariance error-tube visualisation in Chapter~6.
    \item \textbf{Python (Skyfield / SGP4):} For preprocessing raw TLE formats into altitude, inclination, and orbit-class metrics consumed by the front-end.
\end{itemize}
